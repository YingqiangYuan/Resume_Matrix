## The Four Dimensions

### Industry: U.S. healthcare data analytics is a three-layer stack of EHR giants, mid-tier analytics players, and regional specialists

U.S. Healthcare Data Analytics / Digital Health Software Vendors is a mature growth B2B SaaS industry with a 2024 market size of roughly USD 95 to 105B and a CAGR of 8 to 10 percent [01-industry §3]. Structurally it has three layers. EHR giants Epic and Oracle Health (formerly Cerner) own the underlying transaction systems with gross margins of 60 to 75 percent. Mid-tier analytics players such as Innovaccer, Health Catalyst, and Arcadia sit on top of EHRs and provide BI platforms with gross margins of 45 to 60 percent. Regional vendors (such as Cascadia) defend their local markets through geographic depth, customer relationships, and industry know-how. The 2009 HITECH Act drove EHR adoption and catalyzed the first wave of growth. The 2020 ONC interoperability rule (mandatory FHIR adoption) opened the door for third-party applications to access clinical data [01-industry §5]. The 2024 to 2026 wave is agentic AI and natural-language BI agents, and Cascadia Insight Assistant sits squarely in that category. The regulatory framework (HIPAA, HITECH, TJC, CMS value-based purchasing) is both a moat and a barrier to entry. See [01-industry.md](01-industry.md).

### Company: Cascadia is a PNW regional hybrid of Innovaccer plus Health Catalyst

For FY25, Cascadia reports roughly $280M in revenue, about 80 customers, an average ACV of $3.5M, and 1,500 employees. About 30 percent of equity has been held by PE since 2019, and as a private company there is a likely IPO or secondary private transaction window in the next 3 to 5 years (Cascadia 2025 Annual Report, illustrative). Compared with Innovaccer (1,600+ customers nationwide, $200M+ ARR, $3.2B valuation) and Health Catalyst (1,000+ customers, $310M revenue, publicly traded), Cascadia has an order of magnitude fewer customers but a meaningfully higher per-customer contract value. It runs a "few but deep" playbook [02-company §1]. Insight Assistant, launched in 2025, has 12 active deployments with a FY26 target of 30, and is the core of the current growth narrative. Reverse-stress signal: total hospital beds across the PNW region come to roughly 70,000. Even 100 percent penetration caps Cascadia near $400 to 500M in annual revenue, and cross-region expansion would put them in direct collision with Innovaccer and Health Catalyst in the existing Texas and Midwest markets [02-company §1]. See [02-company.md](02-company.md).

### Role: AI Solutions Engineer is the Forward Deployed Engineer pattern localized to healthcare AI

The role is essentially a blend of Solutions Engineer, applied AI knowledge, and on-site customer engagement. It took shape from 2020 to 2023 as Palantir's Forward Deployed Engineer model spread, and matured into a standalone track after the 2024 to 2025 generative AI application boom [03-role §1]. Day to day, expect 40 to 50 percent writing code (Strand Agents, AWS CDK, Python, SQL), 20 to 25 percent on customer sites working with Charge Nurses, Floor Managers, and Clinical Operations Directors, 10 to 15 percent writing English-language documentation aimed at compliance officers, and the rest in internal review and evaluation iteration [03-role §3]. The Cascadia team reports into Customer Success rather than Product Engineering. That placement is similar to Palantir putting FDEs in the Delivery track, and the implication for time-to-Staff promotion should be priced into onboarding expectations [03-role §1]. Entry $95 to 120K base, Mid $145 to 175K, Senior $200K+. AI automation exposure: programming tasks are high (per the Anthropic Economic Index), while customer-facing conversations and business translation work are low [03-role §5, §8]. See [03-role.md](03-role.md).

### Market: PNW AI Solutions Engineer demand is concentrated in Seattle, K-shaped overall but AI roles bucking the trend

A LinkedIn search for "AI Solutions Engineer" in the Seattle area returns 600+ openings [04-market §1.1]. Expanded to the full PNW that number reaches about 1,100, with Seattle alone holding 55 to 65 percent of the open requisitions in this job family [04-market §1.1]. Indeed Hiring Lab data shows overall U.S. tech hiring is down 19 percent versus the 2020 baseline and entry-level openings are down 25 percent, but AI developer roles have more than doubled against that trend [04-market §2]. The $95 to 120K base Cascadia offers sits at P30 to P45 of PNW entry-level tech compensation (well behind Amazon and Microsoft's $155 to 185K total comp), but within the healthcare AI vendor niche it lands at P45 to P55, near the median [04-market §3]. BLS projects 17 percent growth for the software developers occupation from 2024 to 2034, with healthcare IT subsegments growing faster [04-market §4]. AI automation exposure curve for Solutions Engineer: high writing and communication content plus high customer-facing content means short-term exposure is lower than for pure programming roles [04-market §6]. See [04-market.md](04-market.md).

## Consolidated Blind Spots Across Files

The six items below are "unable to verify" flags that show up across all four documents, ordered by importance for interview prep and decision making. Each one comes with a concrete person you can ask.

1. **Customer-Embedded Engineering team size and reporting structure** (who the VP is, headcount, ratio to Core Product Engineering headcount), flagged unable to verify in both 02 §9 and 03 §6. Ask the hiring manager directly in the interview: "What is the team's current headcount, attrition and net hiring over the past 12 months, and the FY26 hiring plan."
2. **Engineer-to-deployment ratio assumption for the 12 to 30 deployment expansion** (how many deployments does one AI Solutions Engineer carry concurrently?), inferred from 02 §6 and 03 §3 with no direct evidence. In an info chat ask: "In the past 12 months how many concurrent customers has a new grad SE typically owned, and what is the average ramp-up time per customer?"
3. **PE owner (30 percent stake) exit timeline and IPO window**, flagged unable to verify in 02 §1 and 02 §11. PE hold periods are typically 5 to 7 years, so a 2019 entry implies a 2024 to 2026 exit window. An IPO would affect employee RSU liquidity. In a face-to-face round you can ask the hiring manager indirectly: "What is the company's funding posture today and are any major capital structure changes expected in the next 18 months?"
4. **Real Cascadia Insight Assistant adoption metrics** (NRR, expansion vs new logos, per-customer query volume monthly growth), flagged illustrative in 02 §3 and 02 §6. In the technical round, ask to see a case study or an anonymized product demo with metrics.
5. **Actual distribution of the 2 to 4 days per month of customer travel** (is it fixed regional routes within the PNW? bunched into one 4-day week or spread across the month?), flagged unable to verify in 03 §3 and 03 §6. At the end of the hiring manager interview, ask: "Over the past three months, what was your team's average travel days per month, and do you have any out-of-state customers outside the PNW?"
6. **Internal AI tool (Copilot / Cursor / Claude Code) deployment ratio and policy at Cascadia**, flagged unable to verify in 03 §8. Customer-Embedded Engineering relies heavily on documentation and code generation, so internal AI tooling directly shapes the learning curve for the first three years. During a break in the technical round, politely ask: "What does the team currently use to write Python and SQL, and does the company sponsor Copilot licenses?"

1. **As interview due diligence material**: the "events from the last 12 months" list in 02 §6, the "entry / mid / senior breakdown" in 03 §5, and the Cascadia salary P30 to P45 range in 04 §3 are all things you can verify (or refute) directly with the hiring manager. Use them to pressure-test your initial "Cascadia is the right direction" conclusion.
2. **Find current or former Cascadia alumni for info chats**: copy the six blind spots above into a question list, find one or two University of Washington or Seattle PNW health IT alumni on LinkedIn, and spend 30 minutes cross-checking. The Customer-Embedded Engineering team is small, so finding a current SE is moderately difficult.
3. **Compare against 1 or 2 alternative directions**: run the same skill set against (a) the equivalent SE role at Innovaccer or Health Catalyst, (b) Snowflake or Databricks Solutions Engineer roles in Seattle, or (c) Microsoft Health and Life Sciences or Amazon Health healthcare AI engineering roles. After comparing all three sets of data, confirm whether the anchor is still Cascadia.
4. **Treat Python + SQL + Strand Agents as the pass bar**: the minimum technical bar is laid out in 03-role §2 (one production-grade Python code sample, SQL including window functions, at least one hands-on RAG evaluation). The minimum readable mental model for the business context (HIPAA, TJC handoff, FHIR, Charge Nurse workflow) is in 01-industry §6 and 03-role §1.
5. **This report does not make the decision for you**: facts only is the foundational principle. Whether to anchor the next several months on Cascadia is entirely the student's call. The report gives you tools to see the current state and the 3 to 5 year direction clearly. It is not a verdict.
