# examples overview, the full main line for running your resume rewrite as an engineering project

> This is the entry point for the examples series. The 13 lessons really form one main line: first see the rules of the resume game clearly, then manage your resume like an engineering project, and let AI handle the hardest few steps for you. This README helps you build the global mental model so you can pick where to jump in. Reads in under 5 minutes.

## 1. Two things this course assumes you already have

First: you already know what kind of role you want to apply for, and you can find concrete Job Descriptions (JDs). That part is covered by the prerequisite course `learn_career_planning`. It walks you through career direction exploration, picking a Job Family, and finding specific JDs on hiring sites.

Second: you already know how to use `understand-landscape` to break down a JD. Given a JD, you can explain the tech stack behind the role, the business scenarios involved, and what the hiring team cares about. You also know how to reverse-expand a few bullet points from your resume into a case document, the long-form version of an experience. Both skills are trained in the prerequisite course.

If you don't have those two things yet, go finish the prerequisite course first, then come back to examples.

---

## 2. The main line of examples, a loop that keeps turning

Before you officially start, keep this loop in your head. It runs through all 13 lessons:

> See the rules → pick a direction (JD) → prepare material (case) → design or elevate a project → compress into bullets → reverse-engineer the Summary → derive submission variants → receive feedback → start the next round

The trickiest steps in this loop are all backed by AI. After finishing examples you'll be familiar with these 10 skills:

| Bottleneck | Matching AI skill |
| :--- | :--- |
| Diagnose your gap with the target role | `qualify-gap-analyze` |
| Design a project you can put on your resume | `mini-project-design`, `mini-project-review` |
| Fill the skill gap | `qualify-execution-plan`, `qualify-coach`, `qualify-mock-interview` |
| Compress a 5000-word case into 3 or 4 bullets | `bullet-writer`, `bullet-reviewer` |
| Reverse-engineer a positioning statement for each direction from your bullets | `summary-writer`, `summary-reviewer` |

The 13 lessons are laid out in the order of this loop. Below is a stage-by-stage walkthrough.

## 3. Stage one, see the nature of the resume game (01 to 04)

Before you write, you need to know who you're writing for. This stage involves no hands-on writing, but every concrete writing tactic later builds on it.

- [01-hiring](./01-hiring/README.md) The three paths through hiring (campus recruiting at big companies, direct apply at small and mid-size companies, experienced hire), and what Hiring Manager / Recruiter / ATS each look at.
- [02-ats](./02-ats/README.md) Tear apart the five big myths about ATS and establish the Reader-Compliant principle: write for a human who scans for five seconds, not for an imagined algorithm.
- [03-new-grad](./03-new-grad/README.md) When new grads don't have formal work experience, how to use the Projects section with Action + Technical Detail + Impact to carry a qualifying resume.
- [04-experienced](./04-experienced/README.md) For people with 1 to 5 years of experience, how to shift from "what I did" to "what I accomplished," push Work Experience up, and drop Education down.

---

## 4. Stage two, treat your resume like an engineering project, one Master Resume plus N submission variants (05)

[05-resume-matrix](./05-resume-matrix/README.md) is the backbone of this method. In the AI era the lines between roles are blurring fast, and the same candidate often fits several Job Families at once. A CS student can absolutely apply to Software Engineer, Data Analyst, and AI Engineer at the same time. Someone with experience might apply to both startups and large companies, spanning healthcare, finance, and consumer internet. This "one person, many directions" submission logic is the same as a creator account matrix on short video platforms. One person, the same core content, produced as multiple variants with different emphasis and posted to different channels, multiplies the chance of being seen.

This lesson tells you: maintain one "ridiculously long" Master Resume as a material library, and derive N submission variants by subtraction only, never addition. No hands-on work in this lesson. It's the scaffold for every AI workflow that follows.

## 5. Stage three, three paths for preparing material (06 to 08)

Bullets are compressed from long case documents. This stage tells you where the case comes from. The essence of rewriting a resume is actually this: design a 3 to 6 month enterprise-grade project a student can finish on their own, precisely matched to the target JD, paired with learning materials and a set of helper agents, where a simple thought experiment can verify whether it can go on the resume. In the past, "designing this kind of project" was the exclusive territory of experienced mentors, because AI can design something that sounds spectacular but a student lacks the judgment to tell good from bad. This method gets the final output to 70 to 80 percent of what an experienced mentor produces, which is good enough for resume purposes.

- [06-prepare-project-material](./06-prepare-project-material/README.md) The map of the three paths: find a mentor to design a project for you, elevate a thin existing experience, or design a new project from zero.
- [07-elevate-existing-project](./07-elevate-existing-project/README.md) The full 6-stage pipeline for path two. Input is an internship or class project that "sounds thin," output is an interview-ready case document. Wired together with `qualify-gap-analyze` → `mini-project-design` → `qualify-execution-plan` → `qualify-coach` → `qualify-mock-interview`.
- [08-design-new-project](./08-design-new-project/README.md) Path three: when you have no relevant experience at all, work backward from the JD to a project that can actually be built. Same 6-stage pipeline, just with the design skill switched to from-scratch mode.

On reading order, 06 is the map and everyone reads it. 07 and 08 are not either-or. If you have both a thin experience worth elevating and want to build a fresh new project, do both 07 and 08. If you can only pick one side, look at whether you have an old experience worth elevating. If yes, go with 07. If not, go with 08.

## 6. Stage four, compress into bullets, then reverse-engineer the Summary (09 to 10)

Once the project is designed and the case background material is collected, only then do you compress everything into resume body text. A great resume is not a pile of bullets stuffed with keywords. It's a complete logical career narrative that clearly shows your skill matrix and growth trajectory. It has to get picked by the machine screening resumes, and make the human interviewer fall for it on a quick scan. Writing a resume like that is hard, and this stage exists to solve it.

Order matters: bullets first, Summary second.

- [09-write-bullets](./09-write-bullets/README.md) Use `bullet-writer` to compress a 5000-word case into 3 or 4 bullets that hold up to follow-up questioning. Introduces the B1 / B2 / B3 / B4 progressive structure, and the same experience can produce one bullet set per Job Family.
- [10-write-summary](./10-write-summary/README.md) Use `summary-writer` to reverse-engineer the Summary from already-written bullets. Write one for each direction and drop them all into the Summary section of the master. The Summary is not autobiography. It's a positioning tag.

Why bullets first? Because bullets are the evidence and the Summary just labels that evidence. A label can be reverse-engineered from evidence. Going the other way, you end up writing empty filler.

## 7. Stage five, actually submit, then keep building up the ammunition stockpile (11 to 12)

- [11-submit-and-collaborate](./11-submit-and-collaborate/README.md) The last mile. Derive a targeted submission variant from the master, collaborate with your mentor through a GitHub plus Google Doc dual track, then export to PDF. Includes layout details like the 70 percent last-line rule that you can only catch with your eyes.
- [12-maintain-new-projects](./12-maintain-new-projects/README.md) After finishing 0 to 1, switch to 1 to N. Every new experience runs through the standard 8 steps, and within a day or two the new project is merged into the ammunition stockpile. After that, each application only takes 30 to 60 minutes. The persona in the Summary also evolves with your career stage, from skill-focused to domain expert.

---

## 8. Stage six, the final loop where the resume becomes a byproduct (13)

[13-final-synthesis](./13-final-synthesis/README.md) introduces no new tools. The task is only one thing: lock the previous 12 lessons into a flywheel that truly self-sustains. Design → fill skills → execute → case → bullets → summary → derive → submit → feedback, and the feedback drives the next round of project design. Once the flywheel is spinning, the resume stops being an anxiety event and becomes a byproduct of an engineer's lifelong career management.

---

## 9. How to read this course

For a first pass, going from 01 to 13 in order is the safest, roughly 12 to 16 hours total. If you already have some background, you can jump in by stage. Want to understand the rules of the game, read 01 to 04. Want to start writing immediately, jump to 05 plus 09 plus 10. Want to use AI to design a new project, read 06 plus 08. Want the long game, read 12 plus 13.

After finishing all of examples, the capability you should have is this: given any JD, you know which skill to use next, what file to produce, and how much time to spend. The resume is no longer a last-minute scramble. It's an engineering project you can keep running indefinitely.
